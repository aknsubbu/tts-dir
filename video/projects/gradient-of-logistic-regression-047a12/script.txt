[model]
Logistic regression turns an input x into a score, <mark name="z"/>z, which is the weights times x plus a bias. The <mark name="sig"/>sigmoid squashes z into y hat, a probability between zero and one.

[loss]
The loss is binary cross entropy. It compares y hat with the true <mark name="label"/>label y, which is zero or one, using <mark name="logs"/>logarithms of y hat and one minus y hat.

[puzzle]
Differentiating logs of a sigmoid sounds messy. Yet the gradient with respect to the weights is just <mark name="dest"/>y hat minus y, times x. Let's see why the mess <mark name="why"/>collapses.

[chain]
The weights reach the loss through z and then y hat, so the chain rule gives <mark name="three"/>three links. The last link is <mark name="easy"/>simply x. The puzzle lives in the <mark name="two"/>first two, which together are the derivative of L with respect to z.

[link1]
For the first link, the derivative of a log is one over its argument, so we get <mark name="fracs"/>two fractions: minus y over y hat, plus one minus y over one minus y hat.

[link2]
Second link. The sigmoid's derivative is a standard fact, which we just state: <mark name="deriv"/>y hat times one minus y hat. Those two factors are <mark name="match"/>exactly the two denominators from the first link.

[term1]
Now multiply. In the first term, y hat on the bottom <mark name="cancel"/>cancels y hat on the right, leaving <mark name="left"/>minus y times one minus y hat.

[term2]
In the second term, one minus y hat <mark name="cancel"/>cancels instead, leaving <mark name="left"/>one minus y, times y hat. No fractions remain.

[expand]
Expand the brackets. We get <mark name="four"/>four terms, and the two products y times y hat <mark name="kill"/>cancel each other. What survives is <mark name="result"/>y hat minus y.

[check]
Try it with label one and prediction zero point eight. The first link is <mark name="a"/>minus one over zero point eight. The second is <mark name="b"/>zero point eight times zero point two. Their product is <mark name="c"/>minus zero point two, exactly y hat minus y.

[final]
Multiply by the last link, x, and the gradient is <mark name="grad"/>the error times the input, the same form as linear regression. For the <mark name="bias"/>bias it is just the error. <mark name="pieces"/>Cross entropy's fractions are built from exactly the pieces the sigmoid's derivative supplies, so they cancel.
